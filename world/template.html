Ride hails

Simulated riders are hailing a robotaxi. Take a hail, then pick a car from the simulated fleet and send it yourself, or let Claude dispatch. You'll watch the car fly to the rider and then take them where they're going. Pads get busy and storms happen, and you only get forecasts. Your score compares what actually happened with the best nearby car, and with the arrival you promised.

The fleet

Seven simulated Starfare cars, each parked at a station or a moon. Every hail starts with a car leaving where it's parked.

Scoreboard

Best score per hail for everyone who shares this page. Each entry says who dispatched the car: Claude, or the player alone.

In the sky now

Everyone who has this page open shares one sky. Their rides show up on the map as they book them. Simulated fleet cars fill the rest, and they're marked as simulated.

Stations

Ride to a station and park there. Real stations use their real orbits from NASA JPL Horizons. Fictional ones are made up for the game, and they're labelled.

How a ride is checked

The dispatcher talks

An AI dispatcher (Claude, through your own Claude account) reads your request and calls the planner. It can't make up a route.

The planner solves

Lambert transfers between real planet positions from NASA JPL's DE421 ephemeris, with patched-conic burns to leave and arrive.

The checker re-flies

An n-body simulation (Sun, 8 planets and the Moon) re-flies the route. It passes only if the final miss is under 1 km and the correction is under 500 m/s.

The gate strikes

Any number in the dispatcher's reply that no tool printed gets struck out on screen. Only rides that pass the re-fly can be booked.

Sky time runs 86,400 times faster than real time, so one real second is one day. Each season covers 2026 to 2046 and then the sky resets. In ride hails, pad traffic, solar flares, Mars dust storms, Titan haze and busy fleet cars are all simulated and seeded per season, so everyone in a season flies through the same ones; they aren't real forecasts.

Starfare is a concept demo by Alvin Impang. It's a made-up robotaxi service with simulated riders and a simulated fleet, and it isn't affiliated with any real company. Burns, battery and fare come from stated, made-up car and thruster models. Landing, aerobraking, life support and orbital plane changes aren't priced.

Planet positions: NASA JPL DE421. ISS and Tiangong orbits: JPL Horizons, 2026-10-16. The dispatcher runs on Claude through each visitor's own account, and it's built so a Grok dispatcher can replace it.
