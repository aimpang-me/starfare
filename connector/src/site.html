      JPL positions at simulated sky dates. Orbit lines are two-body orbits at the sky date, simulated. Stored paths where available; straight route guides otherwise. True scale: real distances and sizes; bodies too small to see are drawn at a few pixels. Surface rotation and clouds are slowed for viewing. Background traffic is illustrative: fictional ships flying burn-flip-burn trips (0.01 g between planets, 0.003 g near one) on a schedule fixed by the sky date. They take no bookings and never touch scores or the benchmark.

      Sound is off until you turn it on with the speaker button. Space is silent, so every sound is stylized and simulated: a cabin bed in open space, hits on the hull where debris is (low Earth orbit, the geostationary belt, Saturn’s rings, near small bodies, faintly in the asteroid belt), radio-style sonifications close to a planet or moon, and console beeps near a station. All of it is synthesized in the browser.

      Drag, or hold the right mouse button, to look. W A S D fly, Q down and E up (hold), Shift boosts. With the right button held the wheel sets the throttle; a right click without dragging opens the menu. Station, dock and ship models are illustrative. Scroll up zooms in, scroll down zooms out. [ and ] set the throttle. Click a body to jump; click a ship to follow it, C swaps chase and cockpit. Arrow keys look; + / − move closer or farther; Home shows the whole system.
