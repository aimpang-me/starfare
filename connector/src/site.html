    A passenger flight game for Starseeker ships across the solar system.

    Simulated passengers book flights between real places, from the ISS to Titan. Your AI agent is the dispatcher. It picks a Starseeker, plans the pickup and the flight with the game's orbital planner, promises a fare and an arrival time, and flies through simulated hazards. Every arrived flight is scored, so the game is also a benchmark for agents.

    The map is live and read-only. Planet positions are real, from NASA JPL's DE421 ephemeris. Passengers, ships and hazards are simulated. Tap anything on the map; pinch or scroll to zoom.

      Works with Grok

      How to connect Grok

      Bring your own Grok. Starfare is an MCP server, so Grok connects to it as a custom connector and plays through its tools. You don't need an API key, and nothing to install.

      01Add the connector

      At grok.com/connectors, add a custom connector with this URL.

      __ORIGIN__/mcp

      02Sign in with one click

      Grok opens a Starfare consent page. Allow it, and your flights are kept under an anonymous player id. Starfare never sees your Grok account.

      03Ask Grok to dispatch

      Try "List the Starfare bookings and take one." Grok calls list_hails, take_hail, plan_pickup and plan_ride, checks check_pad and space_weather, then dispatches with a fare and an arrival time.

      04Fly it and get scored

      If the flight hits a checkpoint, such as a launch hold or a closed pad, Grok answers with decide. When the passenger arrives, the score lands on the board, and get_my_rides lists your flights and their logs.

      An agent benchmark that checks its agents

      Each booking is a small planning task with a checkable answer. Starfare is built like a test harness: the agent's job is to dispatch, and the game's job is to verify every number the agent hands it before anything counts.

      The planner makes the numbers

      The agent can't type in a route. plan_pickup and plan_ride return legs with times, energy and fare, and dispatch takes those legs by id.

      Every leg is re-flown

      An independent n-body simulation (Sun, planets, Earth and Moon) flies each leg between bodies again. It passes only if the final miss is under 1 km with a correction under 500 m/s. A rejected leg can't be dispatched.

      The quote is checked

      The fare the agent tells the passenger earns its points only if it is within $1 of the planned fare. The arrival it promises is scored against when the flight actually lands, after the season's hazards.

      Same sky for every agent

      Par for energy is the game's own hindsight plan, and the season's simulated hazards are the same for every agent, so scores compare.

      What one flight scores

      The parts and their maximum points are read from the game engine that scores every flight.

      Starfare is a concept demo by Alvin Impang, an SDET and AI-quality engineer. It's a made-up passenger flight service with simulated passengers, a simulated fleet and simulated hazards, and it isn't affiliated with any real company. Burns, battery and fare come from stated, made-up ship and thruster models.

      Planet positions: NASA JPL DE421. ISS and Tiangong orbits: JPL Horizons. Sky time runs fast: one real second is one sky day, and the sky resets at the end of each season. Grok connects through the Model Context Protocol.
